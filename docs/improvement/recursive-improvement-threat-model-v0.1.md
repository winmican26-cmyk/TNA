# TNA Recursive Improvement Governance v0.1 Threat Model

Status per category: MITIGATED, PARTIALLY MITIGATED, or NOT MITIGATED (by design or by scope).

| # | Threat | Status | Detail |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Candidate manufactures its own authority | MITIGATED | `AuthorityExpansionRequest` is a separate, explicit record type — `authorityWithinCeiling()`/`computeAuthorityDelta()` never infer an expansion from the candidate's own spec; `evaluatePromotion` REJECTs unconditionally when the candidate's authority profile exceeds its ceiling. Tested: `mutation-boundary.test.ts`, `promotion-evaluator.test.ts`, E2E B |
| 2 | Candidate widens its authority via a permissive spec field | MITIGATED (real bug fixed) | `classifyMutation`'s control-plane/evaluator-path detection is computed unconditionally from `DEFAULT_FORBIDDEN_MUTATION_PATH_PREFIXES`/`EVALUATOR_PATH_PREFIX`, ignoring whatever the spec's own `allowed_mutation_paths` claims — a low-class spec cannot legitimize a forbidden path by listing it as allowed. This was a real bug discovered and fixed during this volume's build. Tested: `mutation-boundary.test.ts` |
| 3 | Candidate mutates the evaluator that judges it | MITIGATED | `EVALUATOR_PATH_PREFIX` (`packages/improvement-evaluator/`) is flagged `evaluator_changed` unconditionally by `classifyMutation`, regardless of improvement class or declared mutation scope; `evaluatePromotion` REJECTs on `evaluator_changed` before any other signal is even considered. Tested: `mutation-boundary.test.ts`, `promotion-evaluator.test.ts`, E2E C |
| 4 | Candidate mutates Gate/Sentinel/Auditor/Ledger/deployment-ops rules | MITIGATED | same unconditional `classifyMutation` control-plane detection; REJECTs for any class below CLASS_4. Tested: `mutation-boundary.test.ts` |
| 5 | Candidate self-approves its own promotion | MITIGATED | `CLASS_POLICY.allow_self_promotion` is `false` for every class with no override path; the real `Gate` (unmodified, Volume 1) enforces that the candidate is never a valid approver principal — only a genuine `{kind:'approver'}` principal can approve a HELD operation. Tested: `gate-integration.test.ts`, E2E G ("candidate cannot be its own approver") |
| 6 | Candidate removes/weakens its own required tests | MITIGATED | `detectTestManifestTampering` fires on removal (test_id absent from the after-manifest, independent of hash) or on a hash swap (when the before-entry's `content_hash` is non-null); `evaluatePromotion` REJECTs unconditionally on `testTamper.tampered`. Tested: `mutation-boundary.test.ts`, E2E D |
| 7 | Candidate benchmark-games / memorizes a public benchmark | PARTIALLY MITIGATED (narrow, documented class) | the reward-hacking holdout fixture (`tests/improvement/reward-hacking.test.ts`, `improvement/fixtures/reward-hacking/`) demonstrates that requiring a hidden holdout benchmark alongside the public one defeats literal-memorization gaming, since `benchmarksAllPass` requires every configured benchmark to pass. This detects the implemented benchmark-gaming class only — it does **not** solve reward hacking generally (adaptive gaming that generalizes across both benchmarks, proxy-metric exploitation, or specification gaming outside the benchmark-score channel are all out of scope and not claimed as mitigated) |
| 8 | Candidate forges its own benchmark score | MITIGATED | `runPromotionEvaluation` always spawns the real benchmark command itself and parses real stdout — a candidate cannot pass a self-reported score, since none is ever read from the candidate's spec or claims. The mandatory adversarial test (candidate router hardcodes/claims a perfect score; the real evaluator measures the real, lower number) is exercised by the reward-hacking fixture's second test, where the memorized candidate's real measured holdout score (0.25) — not any claimed score — is what the evaluator records and rejects on |
| 9 | Candidate forges capability/authority delta | MITIGATED | `computeCapabilityDelta`/`computeAuthorityDelta` are pure functions over the CALLER-supplied parent/candidate profiles, not the candidate's own self-report; in the real governor flow, the operator (never the candidate) supplies `parentCapabilityProfile`/`candidateCapabilityProfile`/`candidateAuthorityProfile` to `/evaluate`. `computeCapabilityDelta` is conservative-by-default: any gain not explicitly in `allowedGrowth` is flagged unexpected. Tested: `mutation-boundary.test.ts`, `abuse-cases.test.ts` |
| 10 | Candidate forges Ledger lineage / fabricates a promotion history | MITIGATED | every `IMPROVEMENT_*` event is appended by the trusted governor's own `improvementWriter()` identity (`appendImprovementEvent`), never candidate-writable; `reconstructImprovementGeneration`/`reconstructImprovementLineage` read only real, hash-chained Ledger events. A candidate has no code path that can append to the Ledger at all |
| 11 | Candidate forges canary evidence | MITIGATED | canary runs are created via `store.startCanaryRun` from the real governor orchestration only, with a real `policy_hash` computed from the generation's own real `spec.canary_policy` (fixed during this closure — previously a constant placeholder string, which would have made two different canary policies indistinguishable in recorded evidence); a real Sentinel session backs every canary (`sentinel_session_id` returned from `/canary`, never fabricated) |
| 12 | Candidate forges rollback evidence / rollback silently reports success | MITIGATED (real bug fixed) | `handleRollback` originally called `completeRollback('ROLLED_BACK', ...)` unconditionally with no real check — fixed to call `verifyRollbackTarget()` (real workspace-existence check) first; an unverifiable target is left `INDETERMINATE`, never fabricated as `ROLLED_BACK`. Tested: `promotion-evaluator` rollback paths, E2E J |
| 13 | Evaluation-profile substitution (candidate or operator promotes under criteria different from what was actually evaluated) | MITIGATED (real bug fixed) | `handlePromote` compares the generation's last-recorded `PromotionDecision.evaluation_profile_hash` against the system's current `activeEvaluationProfileHashBySystem` entry and blocks promotion (409) on mismatch. Two real bugs were found and fixed in this exact mechanism during closure: (a) the `POST /v1/systems/:id/evaluation-profile` route originally wrote the active-hash map under the caller-facing system NAME rather than the real internal `system_id` the rest of the code reads by, silently defeating every staleness check; (b) `handleCreate`'s own system find-or-create logic had the same caller-name/internal-id mismatch bug, which (before the fix) caused a brand-new, disconnected system to be created on every single `propose()` call, defeating recursion-budget accumulation, stale-parent detection, and this same evaluation-profile check simultaneously. Tested: E2E H |
| 14 | Stale-parent silent rebase (a different generation becomes accepted while this candidate is still in flight) | MITIGATED | `handlePromote` compares `gen.parent_generation_id` against the system's CURRENT `accepted_generation_id` and blocks (409) on mismatch — no silent rebase onto whatever is now accepted. Tested: E2E I |
| 15 | Recursion runs away (candidate spawns unbounded further generations) | MITIGATED (real bug fixed) | `RecursionBudget`/`checkRecursionBudget()` is runtime-owned (CAS-updated via `consumeRecursionBudget`), never candidate-controlled. This was NOT enforced at all in the packaged governor's `handleCreate` route until this closure — a real, significant gap, fixed by adding the budget check before any generation record is created. Tested: `store.test.ts`, E2E F |
| 16 | Test-tamper / hash-swap where the recorded baseline itself has no real hash | PARTIALLY MITIGATED (structural limitation, documented) | `detectTestManifestTampering`'s hash-change detection only fires when the BEFORE entry's `content_hash` is non-null — a manifest baseline recorded with a null hash structurally cannot detect a later hash swap (though removal detection is unaffected, since it does not depend on hashes at all). Callers that need hash-swap detection must record a real, non-null baseline hash; this is not automatic |
| 17 | Class 5 (authority/evaluator-affecting) auto-promotes without independent human judgment | MITIGATED | `CLASS_POLICY` for `CLASS_5_AUTHORITY_OR_EVALUATOR` requires both `requires_human_approval` and `requires_independent_review`; `evaluatePromotion` HOLDs until both are explicitly `true`, and `allow_automated_promotion`/`allow_self_promotion` are `false` for every class including 5. Tested: E2E G |
| 18 | Isolated mutation workspace escape (candidate writes outside its own workspace) | MITIGATED | `assertInsideWorkspace` (real-path-resolved containment, not string-prefix matching) guards every workspace path construction in `improvement-core`; `createIsolatedWorkspace` always copies the parent into a fresh, generation-id-scoped directory, never mutating the parent. Tested: `git-worktree.test.ts` ("candidate attempts to escape the worktree root via a symlink or absolute path") |
| 19 | Candidate credential exposure via its own execution environment | MITIGATED | `candidateEnvironment()` strips every secret-shaped `process.env` key (reusing the accepted `SECRET_KEY_PATTERN` scanner precedent from Volume 11) before layering an explicit allowlist; every real spawned regression/security/benchmark command in `runPromotionEvaluation` runs under this stripped environment |
| 20 | Candidate mutates repository-wide Git refs (tags/branches) from inside its own disposable worktree | MITIGATED (real, necessary check — not covered by Git's own isolation) | Git's own worktree isolation protects only the currently-checked-out branch (via "already used by worktree" refusal); it does NOT protect tags or other branches, since refs are repository-wide. `detectGitRefTampering()` is a real, separate, implemented check comparing before/after ref snapshots. Tested: `git-worktree.test.ts` (tag force-move, tag deletion) |
| 21 | Candidate adds a brand-new untracked file invisible to mutation-boundary classification | MITIGATED (real bug fixed) | `git diff --name-status` alone never reports untracked files; `gitDiffNameStatus()` was fixed to additionally run `git ls-files --others --exclude-standard` and merge those paths in as `added`. Tested: `git-worktree.test.ts` |
| 22 | Cross-tenant generation/rollback reference | MITIGATED (real bug fixed) | `initiateRollback` originally never validated that `generationId`/`rollbackTargetGenerationId` belonged to the calling tenant before inserting a rollback record — a caller could reference another tenant's generation id. Fixed by validating both via `getGeneration(tenantId, ...)` (which throws `NOT_FOUND` on cross-tenant reference) before the insert. Tested: `tenant-isolation.test.ts` |
| 23 | Gate envelope retry-limit false-positive blocks legitimate long-running governor operation | MITIGATED (real bug fixed) | the improvement governor's Gate envelope originally set `max_retries_per_action: 3` — since Gate's retry counter is per-action-name for the agent's ENTIRE envelope lifetime (not per-resource), this silently BLOCKed every `authorize_generation`/`build`/etc. call past the 4th distinct generation the governor would ever process. Fixed by raising this to match `max_tool_calls` (1000), sized to the governor's whole operating lifetime rather than a single generation's retry allowance. Discovered via real cross-test interaction while building the E2E A–J suite, not by inspection |
| 24 | Auditor-equivalent assessment fabricates PASS for evidence that was never recorded | MITIGATED | every control in `auditor-integration.ts` returns `INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE` when the specific boolean field it needs is absent from recorded Ledger evidence — never filled with a synthetic PASS. `overall` precedence is FAIL > INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE > PASS, so one missing control anywhere prevents a clean overall PASS. Tested: `auditor-integration.test.ts` |
| 25 | Hardcoded/synthetic authoritative evidence anywhere in the governance path | MITIGATED (audited) | a dedicated audit pass searched the entire Volume 12 codebase for hardcoded PASS/ACCEPT/ALLOW literals, pre-baked benchmark results, and constant capability/authority deltas outside test fixtures. Two real instances of a constant placeholder value feeding into recorded evidence were found and fixed: the canary `policy_hash` (previously the literal string `'policy-hash-placeholder'`/`'canary-policy-hash-demo'`, now a real hash of the generation's own `spec.canary_policy`) and the `handleCreate` system-identity mismatch (see #13). No hardcoded Gate/Sentinel/VAD outcome, benchmark result, or Ledger lineage assertion was found in any non-test code path |
| 26 | Demo/production truth divergence (the demo computes its own separate promotion verdict) | MITIGATED (real gap closed) | the demo (`scripts/demo-recursive-improvement-v01.ts`) originally called `runPromotionEvaluation` and hand-walked `store.transitionGeneration` directly, bypassing the real Gate-authorized HTTP routes, real Sentinel canary sessions, and real CLI role enforcement the packaged governor actually provides in production. It was rewritten to spawn the real, packaged governor process and drive all 6 flows through the real operator CLI binary — the identical orchestration path the packaged smoke test and the E2E A–J suite exercise. One honestly documented residual gap: there is no HTTP route yet to report a real Sentinel canary-health observation, so the demo's rollback flow uses the real, HTTP-reachable operator-triggered rollback path (trigger `MANUAL`) rather than an automatic Sentinel-driven one — the rollback verification, state transition, and Ledger events it produces are still fully real regardless of the trigger reason |
| 27 | Malicious/compromised MCP server or model change during an improvement | NOT MITIGATED (out of scope this pass) | this volume governs the promotion decision pipeline for a candidate's code/config changes; it does not implement independent verification of MCP server behavior or model weight provenance beyond what the candidate's declared `allowed_tool_changes`/`allowed_model_changes` fields and the existing accepted MCP boundary (Volume 10) already provide |
| 28 | Concurrent/racing generation writes overwrite a stronger safety state with a stale weaker one (TNA-33) | MITIGATED | every mutable record (`ImprovementSystem`, `ImprovementGeneration`, `RecursionBudget`, `CanaryRun`, `AuthorityExpansionRequest`) uses CAS-style `state_version` optimistic concurrency; concurrent promote-vs-rollback and canary-success-vs-failure races are explicitly tested. Tested: `concurrency.test.ts` |

## Reward hacking — explicit scope boundary

Item 7 above is deliberately narrow. The holdout/reward-hacking fixture in this volume detects exactly one
gaming strategy: literal memorization of a public benchmark's exact input strings. It does not detect, and
this volume makes no claim to detect:

- Gaming strategies that generalize across both the public and holdout benchmark (i.e. a strategy
  sophisticated enough to fool both fixtures).
- Proxy-metric exploitation unrelated to memorization (e.g. optimizing for a benchmark's measurement
  artifact rather than its intended target).
- Specification gaming outside the benchmark-score channel entirely (e.g. gaming the regression suite, the
  capability-delta computation, or the human-approval process itself).
- Any gaming strategy that a differently-designed holdout fixture would be needed to catch.

**This detects the implemented benchmark-gaming class. It does not solve reward hacking generally. Do not
claim more.**

## The core separation this volume exists to enforce (TNA-72)

Competence-change (a candidate becomes better at its task), authority-change (a candidate's granted
operations/resources/budget widens), and control-plane-change (a candidate touches the very machinery that
governs promotion — Gate, Sentinel, VAD, Ledger, Auditor, the evaluator itself, or deployment-ops) are three
structurally distinct categories. Every mitigation above exists because at least one of these three
categories, left unchecked, would let recursive improvement manufacture its own authority rather than earn
it through a promotion the runtime — never the candidate — actually verified.
